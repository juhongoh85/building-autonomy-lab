01 · Modeling

Building PhysicsModeling building thermal behavior and HVAC performance

Research problem

In existing buildings, the measured variables and the range of operating conditions are limited, so equipment performance is difficult to characterize from collected data alone. We link operational data from building automation systems (BAS) and building energy management systems (BEMS) to physics-based models to predict indoor temperature, building loads, and heat-source energy consumption.

Fault detection and diagnosis from operational data

BEMS operational data are used to diagnose overcooling, where indoor temperature falls below the comfort range. A fault detection and diagnosis (FDD) framework that classifies causes by time of occurrence and operating condition, and derives guidelines for improving operation, was applied to an actual office building.

02 · Digital Twin

Multiphysics Digital TwinHybrid modeling based on physics-based models and operational data

Papers Energy Build. 324 (2024)
Appl. Therm. Eng. (2026)
J. Build. Eng. 120 (2026)

Research problem

Physics-based models struggle to reproduce the characteristics of actual buildings, while data-driven models can lose accuracy under operating conditions they were not trained on. We study digital twins that combine physics-based models with operational data to extend the prediction range even in data-limited settings.

Application: thermal energy storage for cooling

In a heat pump-coupled thermal energy storage (TES) system, keeping the chilled water colder than necessary lowers operating efficiency. By linking models of relative humidity, cooling load, the cooling coil, and TES discharge, we determine the maximum chilled-water supply temperature that still meets cooling demand. Operational data and physics-based models identify the supply-temperature limit and set a daily TES charging temperature matched to operating conditions.

Research Directions

Connecting prediction and interpretation to control decisions

We extend the range of prediction by integrating physics and data, understand operating behavior through physical laws and causal relationships,
and connect both to autonomous control matched to the building's state.

Extending prediction and physics-based interpretation

  • Use physical relationships to improve prediction beyond the training range.
  • Interpret equipment operation and energy use through physical laws and causal relationships.
  • Detect changes in equipment performance and recalibrate the digital twin automatically.

Autonomous decision-making based on building state

  • Study operating strategies that jointly consider energy efficiency, indoor comfort, and equipment constraints, using model predictive control (MPC) and reinforcement learning.
  • Build on our hydrothermal energy project to study operating strategies that link heat pumps, thermal storage, and building loads.
  • Extend control from individual equipment to the integrated operation of buildings and building clusters, aiming for autonomous control that connects state assessment to strategy selection and execution.
